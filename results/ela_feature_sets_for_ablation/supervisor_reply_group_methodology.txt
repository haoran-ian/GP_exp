I constructed the groups using a hierarchical, mechanism-based methodology. First, I classified each ELA feature according to the landscape property it measures, based on its definition: objective-value distribution and dispersion, level-set separability, global meta-model structure, information content and ruggedness, or local/global geometry. I then progressively subdivided these broad concepts to obtain 10, 15, and 20 groups, separating features when they use different models, statistics, classifiers, or thresholds. This makes the partitions nested, so every finer group is a subdivision of a coarser group.

The previous feature-family ablation and permutation-importance results were used only as supporting evidence for deciding which broad families warranted finer separation. They did not determine the membership of the groups; feature membership was based on the ELA definitions and the landscape mechanisms represented. This avoids constructing the groups directly from the same algorithm-performance outcomes used in the subsequent ablation study.
